# Research principles

1. Separate numerical evidence from mathematical proof.
2. Separate solver agreement from ground truth.
3. Separate primal feasibility from gradient validity.
4. Separate local sensitivity from global robustness.
5. Separate lower intervention frequency from improved safety.
6. Treat active-set transitions as first-class experimental regimes.
7. Record negative results and solver disagreements.
8. Use committed, reproducible scenario generators.
9. Require independent verification of every experimental solution used in analysis.
10. Keep exact, smoothed, and finite-difference gradients distinct in all records.
